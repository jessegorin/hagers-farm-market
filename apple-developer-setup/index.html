Setting up your Apple Developer account

Your app will be published on the App Store under Hagers Farm Market's own name. Apple requires the business itself to sign up for a developer account, so this part has to be done by you. It is a one-time setup. Follow these steps in order and you'll be done.

About 30 minutes of your time Then Apple reviews it, usually within a few business days $99 per year, paid to Apple

Your name on the App Store

Signing up as a business means the App Store lists "Hagers Farm Market" as the seller, not a person's name and not Chowly.

You own the account

The account belongs to your business and is billed to you. Chowly is added as a user so we can build and update the app, and you can remove us at any time.

Your business email, not a personal one

The account uses info@hagersfarmmarket.com. If staff or owners change later, the account stays with the business.

Before you start

Have these ready before you open Apple's forms. Some screens can't be edited after you click Continue.

  • Your legal business name, exactly as it's registered with the state (for example "Hagers Farm Market LLC"). Apple does not accept a trade name or DBA. If you aren't sure, check your state formation papers or tax documents.
  • Authority to sign for the business. The person enrolling must be an owner or officer, or be authorized to sign agreements on the business's behalf.
  • Access to the info@hagersfarmmarket.com inbox. Apple emails verification codes there. Send it a test email first and make sure you receive it.
  • Your iPhone and your own mobile number. Apple texts sign-in codes to it. Use a real cell number, not Google Voice or another internet phone number, because Apple rejects those.
  • A payment card for the $99 annual fee. Apple charges it after they approve the business.
  • Your state formation document (Certificate of Organization or Articles of Incorporation), just in case. Apple sometimes asks for a copy that shows the state's filing stamp.

The steps

Six steps, in this order

Steps 1 through 3 are the sign-up. Step 4 is waiting on Apple. Steps 5 and 6 happen after Apple approves you, and they connect the account to Chowly.

  1. 1
    developer.apple.com · D-U-N-S lookup

    Find your D-U-N-S number

    A D-U-N-S number is a free nine-digit business ID from Dun & Bradstreet. Apple uses it to confirm your business is real. Many businesses already have one and don't know it.

    1. Go to Apple's lookup tool at developer.apple.com/enroll/duns-lookup.
    2. Enter your legal business name and business address, then search.
    3. If your business appears, write down the nine-digit number. You'll need it in step 3.
    4. If it doesn't appear, submit the request form on that same page. It's free. Dun & Bradstreet can take up to 5 business days to issue the number, and Apple can take up to 2 more days to receive it.

    Your name and address must match exactly what Dun & Bradstreet has on file. Even a small difference, such as "LLC" versus "L.L.C.", can get the enrollment rejected. Use the same spelling everywhere in the steps that follow.

  2. 2
    account.apple.com

    Create an Apple Account with info@hagersfarmmarket.com

    This will be the business's login for everything Apple. Make a new account for it. Don't use a personal Apple Account you already have.

    1. Go to account.apple.com/account and choose Create Your Apple Account.
    2. Fill in the form:
      • First and last name: your own legal name, as it appears on your ID
      • Birthday: your real date of birth
      • Email: info@hagersfarmmarket.com
      • Phone: your own mobile number
    3. Enter the code Apple emails to info@hagersfarmmarket.com, then the code Apple texts to your phone.
    4. Save the password somewhere safe. You'll use it for every step from here on.

    Use your real name and birthday. Apple compares them with the contact details you enter in step 3. A made-up name or a birthday that doesn't match will get the enrollment rejected.

    If Apple won't create the account on the website, try again from your iPhone. Apple is less likely to flag an account created on an iPhone.

  3. 3
    Apple Developer app or developer.apple.com

    Enroll as an organization

    The easiest way is the free Apple Developer app on your iPhone. You can also do it on the website at developer.apple.com/programs/enroll. Sign in with the account you made in step 2.

    1. Choose Enroll Now (in the app) or Start Your Enrollment (on the website).
    2. Your contact information: your legal name, home address, and phone. Check it carefully before you click Continue, because it can't be changed afterward.
    3. For entity type, choose Company / Organization.
    4. Organization information:
      • Legal entity name: exactly as in step 1
      • D-U-N-S number: from step 1
      • Address and phone: the business's, matching Dun & Bradstreet
      • Website: https://www.hagersfarmmarket.com
      • Work email: info@hagersfarmmarket.com
    5. If Apple emails a code to info@hagersfarmmarket.com, enter it.
    6. Confirm that you have authority to sign for the business, accept the agreement, and submit.

    Choose Company / Organization, not Individual. An individual account lists a person's name as the seller on the App Store, and it can't be switched to a business account later without starting over.

  4. 4
    Waiting on Apple

    Let Apple verify your business

    Apple usually reviews an enrollment within a few business days, and sometimes it takes up to a week. During that time:

    1. Answer calls from numbers you don't know. Apple may call the person who signed up to confirm the business.
    2. Watch the info@ inbox for emails from Apple asking for more information, such as a copy of your formation document.
    3. Leave the account alone. Don't change the password, phone number, or security settings until Apple approves you.

    Changing account settings during the review can restart it. This is the most common reason an enrollment ends up taking weeks. Wait until you have Apple's approval email.

  5. 5
    developer.apple.com · appstoreconnect.apple.com

    Pay the fee and accept Apple's agreement

    1. When Apple emails that your business is approved, follow the link to pay the $99 annual fee.
    2. Watch for the "Welcome to the Apple Developer Program" email that confirms you're enrolled.
    3. Sign in at appstoreconnect.apple.com and accept the agreement Apple shows on the home screen.

    App Store Connect won't do anything until that agreement is accepted. You can't invite users or set up the app before then.

  6. 6
    App Store Connect · Users and Access

    Invite Chowly and turn on API access

    1. In App Store Connect, open Users and Access.
    2. Click Add User (the + button).
    3. Fill in the invitation:
      • First name: Chowly
      • Last name: Access
      • Email: app-manager@chowlyinc.com
      • Role: Admin
      • Additional resources: check Access to Cloud Managed Developer ID Certificate
    4. Click Next, then Invite.
    5. Still in Users and Access, open the Integrations tab. If you see a Request Access button, click it and agree to the terms. Only the account owner can do this, and it lets Chowly set up automatic app updates.

    Select the Admin role, not App Manager. Apple has a role called "App Manager," which is easy to pick by mistake because our email address starts with app-manager@. That's only the name of our mailbox. The role must be Admin.

    Copy and paste the email address rather than typing it. If there's a typo, the invitation has to be cancelled and sent again.

Last thing

Send us your Team ID

Sign in at developer.apple.com/account, open Membership Details, and copy the Team ID. It's a 10-character code such as A1B2C3D4E5. Email it to your Chowly contact and let them know the invitation is sent. From there, Chowly handles the rest.
